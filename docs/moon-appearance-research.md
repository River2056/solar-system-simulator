# Moon Appearance and Shape Research

Implementation-oriented notes for replacing the simulator's generic moon sphere/texture. Sources are first-party NASA Science pages and NASA/JPL imagery. Colors describe a plausible visible-light rendering; enhanced-color products are identified so they are not mistaken for unaided-eye color.

## Recommended visual treatment

| Moon | Physical shape | Dominant visible colors | Distinguishing features to encode in Three.js | Primary NASA source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Earth's Moon** | Spherical, with only subtle global relief; use a sphere rather than an irregular mesh. | Charcoal gray overall. Contrast lighter gray highlands with broad, darker gray maria. | Dense overlapping impact craters; bright/high, rough highlands; smooth dark basaltic maria; occasional bright-ray craters such as Tycho. A normal/displacement map should emphasize crater rims rather than exaggerate the globe's silhouette. | NASA says the rocky surface is cratered and pitted, nearly all is covered by charcoal-gray regolith, and the light highlands contrast with dark lava-filled maria: [NASA Moon Facts](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/). |
| **Phobos** | Strongly irregular, lumpy/triaxial body (about 27 × 22 × 18 km), not a sphere. | Very dark charcoal to blackish gray-brown, with subtle tonal variation and darker slope streaks. | Huge **Stickney crater** occupying roughly half one face; long grooves/gouges; heavy small-crater coverage; loose powdery regolith, boulders, landslide streaks. Use a low-poly/asymmetric base mesh plus crater displacement. | NASA gives its three unequal dimensions, identifies Stickney and the grooves, and describes blackish carbonaceous-chondrite-like material, powdered regolith, and dark landslide trails: [NASA Phobos](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/moons/phobos/). |
| **Deimos** | Small, irregular/lumpy, but visibly smoother and less angular than Phobos. | Dark charcoal gray to muted gray-brown. | Heavily cratered but softened by a thick dusty regolith that partly fills craters; mostly rounded silhouette; smaller craters; **no prominent Phobos-like grooves or ridges**. | NASA describes Deimos as small and lumpy, less irregular than Phobos, dark and C-type-like, with a smoother appearance from partly filled craters and a deep regolith: [NASA Deimos](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/moons/deimos/). |
| **Io** | Nearly spherical. | Highly mottled sulfurous yellow and yellow-orange, with white/pale deposits, red-orange patches, and black/dark volcanic centers and lava fields. Avoid a uniform yellow ball. | Hundreds of volcanoes; dark calderas/paterae; irregular lava flows and lakes; bright plume/frost deposits; very few large impact craters because volcanism continually resurfaces the moon. A high-contrast color/albedo texture matters more than strong crater displacement. | NASA describes Io as the solar system's most volcanically active world, with hundreds of volcanoes, tall lava fountains, molten silicate-lava lakes, and resurfacing faster than large craters accumulate: [NASA Io](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/io/). The page's NASA/Juno imagery provides the visible mottled color reference. |
| **Europa** | Nearly spherical and exceptionally smooth at global scale. | Pale cream, off-white, and light tan ice crossed by reddish-brown/rust lines and mottled darker patches. Enhanced-color versions amplify blue and rust tones; use natural-color imagery for the default palette. | Dense networks of long intersecting **lineae** (ridges/fractures), double ridges, disrupted “chaos terrain,” and only sparse impact craters. Keep geometric displacement low, but make fractures prominent in the texture/normal map. | NASA identifies Europa's icy shell and subsurface ocean: [NASA Europa](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/europa/). NASA/JPL explicitly presents side-by-side natural- and false-color Galileo views useful for palette selection: [Natural and False Color Views of Europa](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/natural-and-false-color-views-of-europa/). |
| **Ganymede** | Spherical. | Brownish gray overall, combining dark brown-gray old terrain with lighter gray/tan bands; fresh craters and ejecta are bright. | Large, sharply separated dark ancient cratered regions and younger bright **grooved terrain**; long tectonic furrows/fault-like structures; many craters with bright ejecta. Encode broad terrain provinces in albedo plus directional grooves in a normal map. | NASA describes the brownish-gray rock/ice mixture, older heavily cratered dark areas, younger tectonically deformed light areas, bright recent impact craters/ejecta, and long structural features: [NASA Ganymede](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/ganymede/). |
| **Callisto** | Spherical. | Dark gray-brown/charcoal background densely speckled with brighter gray-to-white impact scars and ejecta. | The most heavily cratered appearance of the set: craters at nearly every scale, bright-rimmed fresh impacts, and the enormous concentric-ring **Valhalla** impact structure. Preserve an ancient, saturated surface—little smooth or grooved young terrain. | NASA calls Callisto the most heavily cratered object in the solar system and provides spacecraft imagery of its dark, impact-saturated face: [NASA Callisto](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/callisto/). |
| **Mimas** | Slightly ovoid rather than perfectly spherical; NASA notes it is not quite large enough to hold a round shape (about 207 × 197 × 191 km). | Pale neutral gray to off-white water ice, with darker crater interiors and shadowed relief. | Dominant **Herschel crater**, about one-third of the moon's diameter, with a high rim and central peak (“Death Star” silhouette); otherwise densely cratered; fractures/chasmata on the opposite hemisphere. Use a mildly ellipsoidal mesh and make Herschel unmistakable. | NASA gives the ovoid dimensions, icy composition, crater-saturated surface, giant Herschel crater, central peak, and antipodal fractures: [NASA Mimas](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/mimas/). |
| **Enceladus** | Nearly spherical, with small topographic deviations. | Brilliant bright white ice, often slightly blue-white; enhanced-color imagery can show bluish fracture zones. | Extremely reflective shell; some smooth, nearly crater-free resurfaced plains contrasted with older cratered regions; south-polar parallel **“tiger stripe” fractures**; ice boulders and continuous geyser/plume jets. Optional translucent particle plumes would make it immediately recognizable. | NASA describes Enceladus as bright white and the most reflective body, with smooth resurfaced areas, a crater-free south polar region, ice boulders, tiger-stripe fractures, and water/ice jets: [NASA Enceladus](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/enceladus/). |
| **Titan** | Spherical; the solid surface is visually softened/hidden by a deep atmosphere. | From space, a mostly uniform golden orange to amber globe with a diffuse brown-orange haze; a thin bluish limb may appear in processed/atmospheric views. The actual surface is darker brown-gray and is best represented only subtly beneath haze. | Titan's signature is its **opaque layered atmosphere**, not exposed craters. Use a second transparent atmospheric sphere with strong limb haze. If surface detail is shown in an infrared/radar mode, include dark equatorial dune fields and irregular methane/ethane lakes and seas near the poles, plus river channels. | NASA identifies Titan's substantial atmosphere and its clouds, rain, rivers, lakes, and seas of methane/ethane: [NASA Titan](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/titan/). A NASA/JPL natural-color Cassini view shows the orange haze that hides the surface: [Titan in Natural Color](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/titan-in-natural-color/). |
| **Miranda** | Approximately spherical but slightly irregular for a mid-sized icy moon; keep the global outline round while allowing strong local relief. | Medium-to-light neutral gray ice/rock, with darker carbonaceous weathering and contrasting light/dark terrain patches. | A chaotic “patchwork” surface: three large **coronae** bounded sharply against older cratered terrain, chevron-like ridges, valleys, enormous fault canyons and cliffs. This moon benefits from stronger displacement/normal mapping than the larger icy moons. | NASA describes the lightly cratered coronae, ridges and valleys, sharp patch boundaries, canyons up to 12 times the Grand Canyon's depth, porous surface, and carbonaceous darkening: [NASA Miranda](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/miranda/). |
| **Titania** | Spherical. | Neutral gray overall, with locally bright frost deposits and darker crater/fault shadows. | Moderate crater coverage cut by very long tectonic fault valleys/troughs in two directions; bright frost along Sun-facing valley walls. The intersecting extensional trough system should be more visually important than fine craters. | NASA describes Titania's neutral gray color, nearly 1,000-mile-long fault valleys, two-direction crustal extension, and highly reflective possible frost deposits: [NASA Titania](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/titania/). |
| **Triton** | Spherical. | Pale pinkish-tan/peach and cream-white nitrogen frost, with subtle bluish-gray or lavender shadows and dark gray-brown plume streaks. | Sparse craters; smooth cryovolcanic plains; distinctive dimpled **“cantaloupe terrain”**/rounded pits and mounds; active nitrogen geysers with long dark wind-blown streaks. Favor broad smooth terrain and subtle pits rather than dense lunar-style cratering. | NASA describes a sparsely cratered surface, smooth volcanic plains, mounds and round pits made by icy flows, reflective nitrogen frost, and active geysers: [NASA Triton](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/moons/triton/). NASA's Voyager 2 global color mosaic is the palette reference: [Global Color Mosaic of Triton](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/global-color-mosaic-of-triton/). |

## Geometry and material priorities

1. **Use non-spherical meshes for Phobos and Deimos**, and a mild ellipsoid for Mimas. The other listed moons can use sphere geometry at simulator scale, with surface relief handled by normal/displacement maps.
2. **Do not share one generic crater texture.** Io, Europa, Enceladus, Titan, Miranda, and Triton are defined by volcanism, fractures, haze, patchwork tectonics, or cryovolcanism rather than uniformly lunar cratering.
3. **Use dedicated atmosphere/plume layers where silhouette cues matter:** orange atmospheric shell for Titan; optional particles for Enceladus and Triton; optional volcanic glow/plumes for Io.
4. **Separate albedo from relief.** Europa's lineae, Io's volcanic deposits, Ganymede's terrain provinces, and Triton's plume streaks are mainly color/albedo cues; Phobos, Mimas, Miranda, and cratered worlds need stronger normal/displacement cues.
5. **Treat enhanced-color imagery carefully.** It is useful for finding boundaries and composition, but the simulator's default visible-light palette should stay closer to NASA natural-color products; an optional “scientific enhanced color” mode could deliberately use the amplified palettes.
